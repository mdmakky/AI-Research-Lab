# Research standards

The rules every skill and agent in this workspace follows. One source of truth: change rules here, not in individual skills.

## 1. Evidence labels
Every substantive statement about a paper or the literature carries one label:

| Label | Short | Meaning | Requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| VERIFIED | [V] | Checked against the paper's own data, tables, figures or results text. | Page ref `(key, p. N)` |
| AUTHOR-CLAIM | [A] | The authors state it; we cannot independently check it (e.g. claims of novelty, interpretations, conclusions beyond the data). | Page ref |
| INTERPRETATION | [I] | Our own reasoning, synthesis or critique. | Say what it is based on |

Page refs use the `=== [PDF page N] ===` markers in `fulltext.txt` (PDF page index, not printed page number). When a quote is used, it is copied verbatim from `fulltext.txt`.

Citation syntax:
- Inside a paper's own `analysis.md`: `(p. N)`.
- Everywhere else (evidence matrix, reports, reviews): pandoc style `[@key, p. N]` or `[@key1; @key2]`. Keys are the folder names under `papers/`. This works with `references.bib` and pandoc.
- `rlab cite-check <project> <file...>` must pass before any written document is handed to the user.

## 2. Never invent
- Every DOI, title, author, year and venue comes from `metadata.json` (resolved by `rlab add` from Crossref/OpenAlex/arXiv/PubMed). Never type a DOI from memory.
- Every finding, number, limitation and quote comes from the paper text. If the paper does not report something, write "not reported".
- Every citation in written output uses an existing paper key in the project. A claim with no source is either removed or labelled [I].
- If something cannot be found or verified, say so plainly. An honest "unknown" beats a plausible guess.

## 3. Source quality tiers
Prefer higher tiers; state the tier when it matters to the argument.

1. Peer-reviewed journal articles and full papers at reputable conferences (`venue_kind: published`); systematic reviews and meta-analyses at the top for evidence synthesis.
2. Peer-reviewed workshop papers, book chapters from academic publishers.
3. Preprints (`venue_kind: preprint`, e.g. arXiv): usable, always flagged "not peer-reviewed". If a published version exists, use it instead.
4. Theses, technical reports, grey literature: background only, flagged.
5. Blogs, news, Wikipedia, vendor material: never cited as evidence. May guide searching.

`venue_kind: published` means the work appeared in a journal or proceedings; it is not proof of rigorous review. Watch for predatory venues (unknown publisher, implausibly fast review, no indexing) and flag them.
Retracted papers (`RETRACTED` flag from `rlab`) are excluded from evidence; mention only as retracted.

## 4. Reading depth
- Detailed methodology, dataset, setup, metrics, findings and limitations require the full text (`evidence_basis: full-text`).
- Abstract-only analysis (`evidence_basis: abstract-only`) may fill objective and headline claims labelled [A] only. All method sections say "full text not available". Abstract-only papers carry less weight in synthesis and gap claims; say so.
- Read the whole paper: methods, results, tables, limitations, appendix if relevant. Note skipped parts in "Analyst notes".

## 5. Critical appraisal checklist
For every empirical paper check and record:
- Sample size and how it was chosen; power or uncertainty reporting.
- Baselines and whether they are fair and current.
- Evaluation design: train/test separation, leakage, cross-validation, held-out data, blinding, control groups.
- Statistical testing: tests, effect sizes, confidence intervals, multiple comparisons.
- Reproducibility: data, code, hyper-parameters, protocol available?
- Conflicts of interest and funding where relevant.
- Whether conclusions go beyond the data (mark those [A] and note it).

Evidence strength: **strong** = adequate N, sound design, replicated or consistent across studies; **moderate** = sound design but single study or limited N; **weak** = small/unclear N, design flaws, abstract-only, or preprint without replication.

## 6. Contradictions
When papers disagree: record both sides in the evidence matrix "Contradictions" section with page refs, then compare data, population, method, metric and time. Offer an explanation only as [I]. Never silently pick a side.

## 7. Research gaps and novelty
A gap is reported only when:
1. It is supported by evidence from analysed papers (explicit future-work statements, consistent limitations, contradictions, untested populations/conditions, missing method comparisons).
2. A dedicated novelty check was run: at least 3 targeted searches (`rlab search`) across at least 2 sources, recent years included, plus a web search for very recent work. Queries, date and closest hits are recorded in the gap entry.
3. Confidence is stated. "No paper found" is phrased as "we found no paper that ... (searches on DATE)", never "this has never been studied".

## 8. Copyright and access
- Download only legal open-access copies: OpenAlex/Unpaywall OA locations, arXiv, Europe PMC, publisher OA pages. `rlab fetch` enforces this and refuses shadow libraries.
- Never bypass paywalls, logins, CAPTCHAs, or rate limits. Never use Sci-Hub, LibGen, Anna's Archive or similar.
- When no OA copy exists: tell the user and ask them to obtain it through their library, then `rlab attach`. Otherwise analyse abstract-only and label it.
- Quotes stay short and attributed. PDFs stay inside the project folder and are not redistributed.

## 9. Preservation
- Original PDFs (`paper.pdf`), extracted text (`fulltext.txt`) and completed analyses (`status: complete`) are never overwritten or deleted without the user's explicit permission. A hook asks the user first (once `setup/claude-settings.json` is installed).
- Analysis status flow: `draft` (in progress) -> `ready` (analyst self-check passed) -> `complete` (critical review passed). Fix review findings while the analysis is `ready`.
- Revisions to a completed analysis go in a new section `## Revision YYYY-MM-DD` with what changed and why, after the user agrees.
- `research-log.md` is append-only.

## 10. Talking to the user (a beginner researcher)
- Progress updates: short, what was done, what was found, what is next.
- Explain research terms on first use in one plain sentence (e.g. "a *systematic review* is a study that collects and evaluates all studies on a question").
- Explain important decisions and their reason: scope cuts, exclusions, weak evidence, gaps.
- Ask only for: scope or research-question choices that change the direction, missing full-text access, paid or restricted resources, and anything destructive.

# Analyse a paper

`rlab` = `python3 scripts/rlab.py`. Rules: [docs/research-standards.md](../../../docs/research-standards.md) — labels §1, never invent §2, reading depth §4, appraisal checklist §5.

Input: project id + paper key. Files live in `research/<id>/papers/<key>/`.

## 1. Prepare
- `rlab scaffold <id> <key>` (creates `analysis.md`; never overwrites an existing one).
- Read `metadata.json`. Note `venue_kind`, `is_retracted`, `fulltext.status`, `fulltext.identity`, `fulltext.text_layer`.
- `identity` UNCONFIRMED → read PDF page 1 and confirm it is this paper; if not, stop and report.
- `text_layer` POOR → read pages from `paper.pdf` with the Read tool (`pages` parameter) instead of `fulltext.txt`.
- No full text → `evidence_basis: abstract-only`; fill objective and headline claims as [A] from the abstract in `metadata.json`, write "full text not available" in sections 3–6, 8 and 10, and stop at step 4.

## 2. Read the whole paper
Read `fulltext.txt` in chunks (Read with offset/limit) from start to end: abstract, introduction, method, results, tables, discussion, limitations, conclusion. Use Grep for `limitation|threat|future work|we did not|sample|dataset|N =` to make sure nothing is missed. For tables or figures garbled by text extraction, read that PDF page directly. Page numbers come from the nearest preceding `=== [PDF page N] ===` marker.

## 3. Write the analysis
Fill every section of `analysis.md`. Each [V]/[A] item ends with `(p. N)`. Use "not reported" when the paper is silent — that absence is itself useful information (e.g. no sample size reported). Separate author-stated limitations (section 8, [A]) from limitations you identify (section 9, [I]). Apply the appraisal checklist to section 10. Fill the evidence table (section 13) with rows linked to project RQ ids from `RESEARCH-QUESTIONS.md`.

## 4. Frontmatter
Fill the single-line frontmatter fields: `design`, `dataset`, `sample_size`, `metrics`, `main_finding` (≤25 words), `relevance` (high/medium/low). These feed the literature matrix.

## 5. Self-check
- Every [V]/[A] has a page ref; `rlab cite-check <id> research/<id>/papers/<key>/analysis.md` passes.
- Grep `fulltext.txt` for 3 of your quotes/numbers: each is found on the cited page.
- No template placeholder or empty required section remains.
Then set `status: ready` and run `rlab index <id>`.

Done when: `status: ready` and the self-check passed — or `status: draft` with the blocking reason written in "Analyst notes".

## 6. Return
Report: key, 2-line summary, evidence-table rows, anything surprising (contradicts other work, suspicious results, missing data).

## Cross-paper observations

1. **Same-family construction or judging, with same-family winners (a recurring threat).**
   - ProgramBench: tests were written by Claude Sonnet 4.5 with source access, and Claude models top the leaderboard [@yang2026programbench, p. 3; p. 31].
   - Vibe Code Bench: Claude Sonnet 4.5 is the judge, and Anthropic and OpenAI models lead; the specs were drafted by GPT-5 and GPT-5.3-Codex is first [@tran2026vibe, p. 3; p. 4; p. 6].
   - MobileForge: Gemini 2.5 Pro is both judge and candidate [@wu2026looks, p. 6].
   - SusVibes: the environment builder is Claude 4 Sonnet, and SWE-agent + Claude 4 Sonnet is best on FuncPass [@zhao2025vibe, p. 7; p. 17].
   - Only MobileForge tests self-preference, and only qualitatively [@wu2026looks, p. 13].
   - The evidence matrix should list this as a shared RQ4 weakness, not repeat it per paper.
2. **Does iterative feedback help? There is an apparent tension.**
   - One-shot self-repair, environment repair and planning did not raise functional scores and lowered non-functional quality [@pan2026functional, p. 8].
   - Browser self-testing correlates with accuracy (r = 0.72 across 16 models) [@tran2026vibe, p. 7].
   - Compiler-feedback loops raise compile success, 74% to 98% [@zhou2025declarui, p. 9], and make build success saturate at 100% [@wu2026looks, p. 12].
   - [I] Possible reconciliation: one-shot interventions vs multi-step agentic loops, and different outcome metrics (non-functional quality vs workflow pass / compilation). Record both sides under "Contradictions" in the evidence matrix (standards §6). Do not pick a side.
3. **Reasoning or "thinking" effort.**
   - "Thinking" models are not consistently better and average slightly below standard ones [@pan2026functional, p. 6].
   - Higher reasoning effort clearly helps GPT and Gemini 3.1 Pro on Vibe Code Bench (20-app subset) [@tran2026vibe, p. 7].
   - These differ in setting (single pass vs agentic) and are not a direct contradiction, but should be noted.
4. **Code size and structure point in different directions depending on the baseline.**
   - Rebuilt CLIs are smaller and more monolithic than the human originals [@yang2026programbench, p. 9].
   - Among models, the visual leader writes 2.2× more code with larger files [@wu2026looks, p. 6].
   - Replit produces about 60% duplicated lines [@mota2026comparing, p. 4].
   - MetaGPT needed explicit modularity prompts to avoid monolithic God classes [@zhu2026understanding, p. 5].
   - "More code is worse" is not established. Both mota (per KLOC) and zhu (raw counts, ρ = 0.94 with TLoC) treat smell counts as size-driven, but only mota normalises.
5. **SonarQube vs PyExamine, and the molison2025llm link.**
   - zhu2026understanding argues that SonarQube issue counts conflate linting with structural smells, and positions itself against Santa Molison et al. (= project key `molison2025llm`) [@zhu2026understanding, p. 2].
   - mota2026comparing relies on SonarQube alone [@mota2026comparing, p. 2].
   - When synthesising RQ3, check molison2025llm's analysis for this disagreement about measurement validity.
6. **Possible data overlap.**
   - Interaction2Code takes 100 of its 127 pages from the C4 validation set, using Design2Code's filtering [@xiao2025benchmarking, p. 2].
   - Design2Code's 484 pages also come from the C4 validation set [@si2025benchmarking, p. 2].
   - The two benchmarks may share source pages, so they are not fully independent evidence on screenshot-to-code fidelity.
   - Both reuse the same Position metric (1 − max(|Δx|, |Δy|)) [@si2025benchmarking, p. 4; @xiao2025benchmarking, p. 4].
7. **Version drift.**
   - Four analyses cite a published DOI but analyse an arXiv version: tran2026vibe (v3), xiao2025benchmarking (v3, later than ASE 2025), zhou2025declarui (v1, earlier than PACMSE), zhu2026understanding (v1, with a different title).
   - zhao2025vibe has the opposite problem: published, but metadata says preprint.
   - Page refs follow the arXiv PDFs in all four cases, so quotes in reports must cite the PDF actually held.
8. **Model overlap allows a weak consistency check.**
   - Claude Opus 4.6 is top-tier in three independent benchmarks [@tran2026vibe, p. 6; @yang2026programbench, p. 31; @wu2026looks, p. 7].
   - GPT-5.2 appears in pan (41.81% functional) and tran (53.50%), and is also the worst-aligned judge in tran [@pan2026functional, p. 6; @tran2026vibe, p. 9].
   - [I] Rankings agree in broad terms, but the metrics are not comparable, so absolute numbers must not be pooled.
9. **Contamination handling is uneven.**
   - Only Interaction2Code runs an explicit check, a BLEU similarity to the original HTML [@xiao2025benchmarking, p. 5], and the analysis rightly calls it weak.
   - ProgramBench uses a different-language ablation [@yang2026programbench, p. 6].
   - RAL-Bench acknowledges exposure [@pan2026functional, p. 2].
   - MobileForge, DeclarUI, SusVibes and Design2Code do not discuss contamination of their own data, even though all use popular public apps or pages.
   - This is a candidate RQ4/RQ5 gap and needs a novelty check before it can be claimed.
