    18	# Open or Frontier? A Cost- and Energy-Aware Benchmark of Large Language Models for Software Vulnerability Detection
    19	
    20	- Authors: Deininger, Patrick; Slany, Wolfgang
    21	- Year / venue: 2026, Computers (published)
    22	- DOI: 10.3390/computers15090623 · URL: https://doi.org/10.3390/computers15090623
    23	- Source text: `fulltext.txt` (page refs = PDF page index)
    24	
    25	Labels: **[V]** verified in the paper's own data/tables/results · **[A]** author claim · **[I]** our interpretation.
    26	Every [V]/[A] item ends with a page ref `(p. N)`. Write "not reported" when the paper does not say.
    27	
    28	## 1. Research objective
    29	[A] To benchmark open-weight against frontier LLMs on function-level vulnerability detection, treating monetary cost and energy as "first-class axes alongside detection quality" (p. 1). The study also calibrates the LLM results against non-LLM reference points (static analysers and learned detectors) "to gauge absolute difficulty" (p. 2). [A] The authors claim the conjunction is new (a security task, a five-model open roster, and cost and energy as per-task axes), "not ... a priority" on any single axis (p. 3).
    30	
    31	## 2. Research questions / hypotheses
    32	- [V] No numbered RQs. Contributions are framed as (p. 2):
    33	  - a cost- and energy-aware open-vs-frontier benchmark on label-clean PrimeVul with non-LLM reference points;
    34	  - evidence on the quality–efficiency Pareto frontier;
    35	  - an account of what can and cannot be measured for closed API models.
    36	- [I] Implicit questions:
    37	  1. Are open-weight LLMs competitive with frontier LLMs in quality?
    38	  2. Are they more cost- and energy-efficient?
    39	  3. How do LLMs compare with SAST and fine-tuned detectors at realistic prevalence?
    40	
    41	## 3. Methodology
    42	- Research design: [V] Empirical benchmark of binary function-level detection ("does this C/C++ function contain a vulnerability?") on a fixed stratified PrimeVul sample (p. 4). Additional analyses (pp. 9–20):
    43	  - re-scoring at the natural 1:44 prevalence;
    44	  - configuration-matched families A/B/C;
    45	  - prompt, sample-draw and run-to-run robustness;
    46	  - a reasoning-vs-direct replication across two measurement epochs.
    47	- Approach / model / intervention: [V] Eight LLMs prompted zero-shot through a unified OpenAI-compatible API gateway (p. 4, Table 2). [V] The gateway is **not named** (pp. 4, 29).
    48	  - **Frontier:** Claude Sonnet 5, GPT-5.1, Gemini 3.1 Pro (`gemini-3.1-pro-preview`); active parameters assumed ~100B for the energy estimate.
    49	  - **Open-weight:** DeepSeek-V3.2 (~37B active), GLM-5 (~32B active), Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen3-Coder-30B (3B active MoE), Gemma-3-4B.
    50	  - [V] Pinned providers and precision: DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5 at GMICloud FP8; Llama-3.3-70B at DeepInfra FP8; Qwen3-Coder-30B at SiliconFlow FP8; Gemma-3-4B at DeepInfra bf16 (p. 29, Table A2).
    51	  - [V] Main configuration: direct answer, reasoning disabled where permitted. Gemini cannot disable reasoning (HTTP 400) (p. 4, p. 29).
    52	  - [V] Prompt: a security-reviewer persona; answer YES/NO first, then an optional explanation (p. 28). A lenient parser counts unparseable outputs as parse failures, not "safe" (p. 4, p. 29).
    53	- Baselines or comparison conditions:
    54	  - [V] **Trivial baselines:** always-safe and always-vulnerable (p. 7, Table 4); always-flag at 1:44 prevalence (p. 8, Table 5).
    55	  - [V] **SAST arm** (p. 6, p. 10, Table 7): Flawfinder 2.0.20 (lexical), Cppcheck 2.21 (flow-sensitive), Semgrep 1.175 with community C and security-audit rulesets (pattern plus intraprocedural taint). A function is flagged if any finding reaches a swept severity threshold.
    56	  - [V] **DL arm:**
    57	    - CodeBERT fine-tuned on Devign (off the shelf, cross-dataset, argmax, 512 tokens) (p. 6);
    58	    - CodeBERT (125M) fine-tuned on PrimeVul's training split, with the threshold selected on the validation split (p. 10–11).
    59	  - [V] **CodeQL was not run.** PrimeVul functions are isolated snippets with no build, so no CodeQL database can be built (p. 10–11).
    60	
    61	## 4. Dataset and sample
    62	- Data source(s): [V] PrimeVul test split (C/C++): 24,788 functions after loading, 549 vulnerable — "a realistic 1:44 class ratio" (p. 4).
    63	- Sample size (N) and unit: [V] N = 1549 functions: all 549 vulnerable plus 1000 seeded-random safe functions (p. 4). [I] Prevalence in the evaluated sample is 35.4% (549/1549). This is **not** 50/50, although the text once calls it "balanced by construction" (p. 8). [V] The effective N per model is 1526–1549 because parse failures are excluded (p. 6).
    64	- Sampling / selection method:
    65	  - [V] Label-stratified sample fixed by seed (p. 4).
    66	  - [V] Two further near-disjoint safe pools (<6% overlap) were drawn for robustness (p. 19).
    67	  - [V] LLM inputs were truncated to 8000 characters. This affects 105/1549 functions and 87/549 (15.8%) of the vulnerable ones; non-LLM baselines see untruncated code (p. 4).
    68	  - [V] All CVEs date from 2008–2022 and predate the models' training cutoffs (p. 24). [V] The PrimeVul training split contains no evaluated function or function body (up to whitespace) (p. 10, p. 25).
    69	- Data availability (public? link?): [V] Released on Zenodo (doi 10.5281/zenodo.21391073): harness, configurations, sampled function IDs, price snapshot, energy sweep, baseline scores and aggregated results (p. 26). [V] PrimeVul is fetched from its original authors by script; the fine-tuned checkpoint is regenerable by script (p. 26).
    70	
    71	## 5. Experimental setup
    72	- Procedure, settings, hyper-parameters, tools:
    73	  - [V] Harness and decoding: a test-driven harness with 82 automated tests; temperature 0, seed 12345, one generation per item; output budget 64 tokens in the matched configuration (256 for two frontier models in the first epoch) (p. 6, p. 29).
    74	  - [V] Cost: computed from logged input/output tokens × list price at a pinned provider, with gateway fallbacks disabled (p. 5).
    75	  - [V] Provider price dispersion: up to 14.4× for DeepSeek-V3.2 (USD 0.209–3.000 per million input tokens) (p. 5, Table 3).
    76	  - [V] Energy (p. 5):
    77	    - FLOP estimate E ≈ 2·N_active·T·ε for API-served models;
    78	    - measured with the NVML energy counter on a dedicated NVIDIA H200 for locally servable open models (concurrency 1, idle-subtracted "active" energy);
    79	    - plus a concurrency sweep c = 1/8/32/64 (p. 14, Table 9).
    80	  - [V] Latency: client-side under concurrency 10, so it conflates queueing (p. 30).
    81	  - [V] Statistics: stratified paired bootstrap of balanced-accuracy differences (20,000 resamples, seed 12,345) with Holm correction over a 9-comparison primary family and a 15-comparison strict family (p. 6, p. 28).
    82	- Code availability: [V] The harness and configurations are released (p. 6, p. 26).
    83	
    84	## 6. Evaluation metrics
    85	- Metric — what it measures — why chosen:
    86	  - [V] **Balanced accuracy** is the primary metric, because accuracy is dominated by the always-safe classifier (0.646 accuracy, above every LLM) and both trivial classifiers score exactly 0.5 on it (p. 6).
    87	  - [V] MCC and F1 are also reported (p. 6).
    88	  - [V] Deployment metrics at 1:44 prevalence are derived from the prevalence-invariant TPR/FPR (p. 8, Table 5):
    89	    - precision; false positives per true positive (FP/TP);
    90	    - alerts and found vulnerabilities per 1000 functions;
    91	    - **USD per true positive** and **kJ per true positive**.
    92	  - [V] Elicited 0–100 confidence gives ROC-AUC where usable. VD-Score is declined because the coarse score grids cannot reach its 0.5% FPR point (p. 9–10).
    93	- Statistical testing (tests used, significance, effect sizes, confidence intervals):
    94	  - [V] Paired bootstrap 95% CIs and Holm-adjusted p for open-vs-frontier gaps (p. 27, Table A1).
    95	  - [V] Wilson-based 95% CIs on precision at deployment prevalence for the non-LLM baselines (p. 10, Table 7).
    96	  - [V] Robustness: Spearman rank correlation across prompts and draws (p. 19, Table 13).
    97	  - [V] Output-budget effects are tested with a paired bootstrap (p. 16, Table 10).

   113	2. **Open vs frontier significance** — [V] DeepSeek-V3.2 significantly beats all three frontier models: +0.054 to +0.060 balanced accuracy, Holm p < 5×10⁻⁴ (p. 6, p. 27, Table A1).
   114	   - [V] Gemma-3-4B beats Claude-Sonnet-5 (+0.037, Holm p = 0.032); the other top-open gaps are not significant (p. 7, p. 27).
   115	   - [V] Qwen3-Coder-30B is indistinguishable from chance (MCC 0.022, CI −0.03 to 0.07) (p. 7).
   116	3. **Budget-matched second epoch (Family A)** — [V] Balanced accuracy / MCC: DeepSeek-V3.2 0.711 / 0.403; GLM-5 0.669 / 0.352; Gemma-3-4B 0.653 / 0.297; Claude-Sonnet-5 0.653 / 0.321; GPT-5.1 0.615 / 0.225; Llama 0.609 / 0.267; Qwen 0.568 / 0.136; Gemini 0.606 / 0.252, on only the 54.2% of tasks it answered (p. 17, Table 11).
   117	   - [V] Claude equals Gemma (+0.0003, p = 0.98) (p. 17).
   118	   - [V] Absolute numbers shifted by up to 0.06 between epochs seven weeks apart ("service drift") (p. 7–8, p. 20).
   119	4. **Reasoning mode** — [V] Reasoning helps every frontier model (Claude +0.032, GPT-5.1 +0.044, Gemini +0.096) but hurts DeepSeek-V3.2 (−0.133, p < 10⁻⁴) and does not help GLM-5 (−0.019, n.s.); it costs 4.5–12.1× more per task (p. 17–18, Table 12).
   120	   - [V] Best observed: DeepSeek 0.711 (direct) vs Claude 0.685 and Gemini 0.680 (reasoning); DeepSeek vs Claude p = 0.081 (p. 17).
   121	   - [V] Reasoning lowers DeepSeek's specificity from 0.52 to 0.37 (p. 20).
   122	5. **Prompt sensitivity dominates** — [V] Two paraphrases move balanced accuracy by up to 0.088 and produce three different winners (DeepSeek 0.711, Claude 0.741, GLM-5 0.700); Spearman ρ vs anchor = 0.381 and 0.810 (p. 19–20, Table 13).
   123	   - [V] Safe-pool redraws move scores ≤0.015 (ρ = 1.000) (p. 19).
   124	   - [V] Temperature-0 verdict agreement is 88.3–100%; only Gemma is exactly reproducible (p. 19).
   125	6. **SAST baselines on the same functions** (p. 7, p. 10, Table 4, Table 7) — balanced accuracy / recall / specificity / precision at 1:44:
   126	   - [V] Flawfinder risk≥1: 0.589 / 0.271 / 0.907 / 6.20%; risk≥4: 0.523 / 0.055 / 0.992 / 13.40%.
   127	   - [V] Cppcheck: 0.548–0.592 balanced accuracy; precision 2.46–3.77%.
   128	   - [V] Semgrep severity≥INFO: 0.514 / 0.031 / 0.998 / 25.96% (CI 5.7–66.9%). It flags 19 of 1549 functions with 17 TP and 2 FP (p. 11).
   129	   - [V] Flawfinder is "ahead of every LLM at every threshold" on deployment precision but finds almost nothing (p. 9, p. 23).
   130	7. **Fine-tuned DL baseline beats every LLM** — [V] CodeBERT-PrimeVul (125M, validation-tuned threshold) reaches balanced accuracy 0.765, MCC 0.526, recall 0.709, specificity 0.821, precision 8.23% at 1:44 (CI 6.9–9.7) and ROC-AUC 0.845 (p. 10, Table 7; p. 10–11). [V] CodeBERT-Devign (cross-dataset) collapses to 0.518, flagging 97% of functions (p. 7, p. 23). [V] At argmax 0.5 the PrimeVul detector predicts all-safe because no score exceeds 0.372. The authors "nearly reported" this artefact as evidence that trained detectors are no better than LLMs (p. 11).
   131	8. **Deployment at 1:44 prevalence** — [V] Every LLM's precision is 2.3–3.8% vs 2.21% for always-flag; FP/TP ranges from 25.6 (DeepSeek) to 41.9 (Qwen); models alert on 43–75% of functions (p. 8, Table 5). [A] "Neither is ready for independent use" (p. 9).

   185	## 8. Limitations stated by the authors
   186	- [A] Energy is measured for only some open models; frontier energy rests on an untested active-parameter assumption and is reported as a sensitivity range (p. 23).
   187	- [A] Configuration asymmetries: Gemini cannot run without reasoning; two frontier models needed larger output budgets in the main run (p. 24).
   188	- [A] Latency is confounded by queueing (p. 24).
   189	- [A] Training-data contamination: all CVEs are 2008–2022 and may appear in pre-training; absolute scores "may be optimistic"; a post-cutoff restriction is planned (p. 24).
   190	- [A] One prompt, one draw and one generation in the headline table; variation across datasets and task formulations remains unbounded (p. 24).
   191	- [A] Serving-layer dependence (p. 24–25):
   192	  - price spread up to 14×;
   193	  - two silent serving faults: billed empty outputs, and a reasoning flag ignored with HTTP 200;
   194	  - quality is robust to provider and precision for Llama (0.599–0.609).
   195	- [A] CodeQL cannot be applied to unbuildable function snippets (p. 25).
   196	- [A] Scope: one dataset, one task formulation, function level only; other languages and statement-level localisation are future work (p. 25).
   197	- [A] "No result here should be read as evidence that function-level vulnerability triage is ready for independent use" (p. 26).

   199	## 9. Limitations we identified
   200	- [I] **Internal inconsistencies from revision** (the paper went through at least one major review round, p. 1, pp. 16–19):
   201	  - Gemma-3-4B is said to be incompatible with the local vLLM/CUDA build, so its energy is FLOP-estimated (p. 6, p. 15). Yet Table 9 reports a measured concurrency sweep for Gemma (p. 14), and the abstract says three open models were measured (p. 1), while p. 5 says two.
   202	  - The limitations list says a fine-tuned PrimeVul detector "is the remaining gap" (p. 25), but Section 4.4 reports exactly such a detector (p. 10–11).
   203	  - The reasoning cost multiplier is "2.5 to 7.6 times" on p. 24 vs 4.5–12.1× in Table 12 (p. 18); these are different epochs, but the text does not say so.
   204	  - The sample is called "balanced by construction" (p. 8) although it is 35.4% positive (p. 4).
   205	  - These are reporting slips, not evidence of wrong numbers, but readers should cite table values rather than prose.
   206	- [I] **The SAST arm is weak by design for C/C++.** Flawfinder, Cppcheck and Semgrep community rules on isolated functions, with no CodeQL, Infer or commercial tools (p. 10–11). The comparison is LLM vs lightweight function-level SAST, not vs semantic whole-program SAST.
   207	- [I] **Not post-cutoff** (CVEs 2008–2022, p. 24), and the measured recall gradient by CVE year suggests memorisation. The LLM-vs-fine-tuned comparison is also asymmetric: the fine-tuned model is in-distribution, while the LLMs are zero-shot (p. 11).
   208	- [I] **Cost per TP excludes triage.** With 25.6–41.9 FP per TP (p. 8), analyst time would dominate compute cost (cents per TP). The paper's USD/TP therefore understates the practical cost per confirmed vulnerability by a large factor.
   209	- [I] **Unnamed gateway** and preview model identifiers (`gemini-3.1-pro-preview`, p. 29) limit long-term reproducibility. The service drift of up to 0.06 between epochs (p. 20) shows API results are time-stamped observations.
   210	- [I] **Function-level, C/C++ only** — no repository context. The truncation at 8000 characters affects 15.8% of positives (p. 4).
   211	- [I] **AI-assisted writing and code.** The authors used Claude Opus 5/Sonnet 5 to draft text and write Python code (p. 27), and Claude-Sonnet-5 is one of the evaluated models. This is disclosed and the data are stated to be the authors' own; we note it for transparency, not as a conflict.

   213	## 10. Threats to validity
   214	- Internal (confounds, leakage, selection):
   215	  - [V] Train/test separation for CodeBERT-PrimeVul is verified: no evaluated function or body is in training (p. 25).
   216	  - [V] Thresholds are tuned on validation, not test (sweeping on test would give 0.777 vs the reported 0.765) (p. 11).
   217	  - [V] Contamination is not controlled but is measured as a recall gradient by CVE year (p. 24).
   218	  - [V] Epoch drift is handled by within-epoch comparisons (p. 16–18).
   219	  - [V] Parse failures are excluded, not recoded (p. 27).
   220	- External (generalisability of sample/context):
   221	  - [V] One dataset (PrimeVul), C/C++, function level (p. 25).
   222	  - [I] It is unclear whether results hold for repository-level detection or other languages.
   223	  - [I] Model versions are a 2026 snapshot.
   224	- Construct (do metrics measure the concept?):
   225	  - [V] Balanced accuracy is chosen deliberately; the paper shows its ranking differs from the precision ranking at deployment prevalence (GPT-5.1 3rd on precision, 5th on balanced accuracy) (p. 8–9).
   226	  - [I] PrimeVul labels are "near-human-accuracy" per its authors (p. 3), but no own label audit was done.
   227	- Statistical conclusion (power, multiple comparisons, variance):
   228	  - [V] Paired bootstrap with Holm correction; families are declared explicitly (p. 28).
   229	  - [V] Prompt variance (≤0.088) exceeds most between-model gaps (p. 19–20). [A] "No single-prompt ranking of LLMs on this task should be read as a property of the models" (p. 20).
   230	  - [V] Deployment precision CIs are reported for the non-LLM rows and the best/weakest LLM (p. 10, Table 7).
   231	- Reproducibility (data/code/setup reported?):
   232	  - [V] Strong: harness, configurations, prompts, parser rules, pinned providers and prices, function IDs and energy data are released (p. 26, pp. 28–30).
   233	  - [V] API non-determinism remains: 0–12% of verdicts flip at temperature 0 (p. 19).

   235	## 11. Future research directions
   236	- Stated by authors [A]:
   237	  - calibrate the FLOP energy estimator against measured batched energy, per architecture (p. 26);
   238	  - corroborate on a second dataset and task formulation (p. 26);
   239	  - post-cutoff CVEs or paired PrimeVul variants to bound memorisation (p. 24);
   240	  - controlled single-request latency (p. 24);
   241	  - other languages, statement-level localisation, and VD-Score at realistic imbalance (p. 25).
   242	- Suggested by us [I]:
   243	  - Add triage time per alert to turn USD/TP into cost per confirmed vulnerability (G6).
   244	  - Run a semantic SAST arm at repository level, where builds exist.
   245	  - Repeat with post-cutoff functions so that the fine-tuned vs zero-shot comparison is contamination-free (G2).

   247	## 12. Relevance to our research
   248	- Research questions addressed (RQ ids): RQ1 (primary: LLM vs SAST vs fine-tuned DL on identical functions), RQ2, RQ3 (cost/energy trade-offs; no hybrid), RQ5.
   249	- How it informs our work [I]:
   250	  - **RQ1:** one of very few same-benchmark three-family comparisons. The ranking depends on the metric:
   251	    - on balanced accuracy: fine-tuned CodeBERT > best LLMs > Flawfinder/Cppcheck > Semgrep;
   252	    - on precision at realistic prevalence: Semgrep (26.0%) and strict-threshold Flawfinder (13.4%) > CodeBERT (8.2%) > lenient Flawfinder (6.2%) > all LLMs (2.3–3.8%). Cppcheck (2.5–3.8%) sits in the LLM range (p. 10, Table 7);
   253	    - all are far from deployable.
   254	  - This fits our "LLMs higher recall, lower precision" pattern and C3/C5.
   255	  - **Correction for our gap report.** The G2 and G6 novelty-check notes describe *Open or Frontier?* as "No SAST". That is **incorrect**: Flawfinder 2.0.20, Cppcheck 2.21 and Semgrep 1.175 are run on the identical 1549 functions (p. 6, p. 10). G2 is therefore narrowed further: a same-benchmark LLM + SAST + fine-tuned DL comparison now exists at function level on C/C++. What is still missing is post-cutoff code, repository level, and semantic SAST.
   256	  - **G6 is narrowed further.** USD/TP and kJ/TP at 1:44 prevalence are reported for 8 LLMs (p. 8). The remaining gap is the triage-effort term and a costed SAST/DL arm (≈0 is not quantified per TP).
   257	  - **G1:** a further study that reports trivial baselines and realistic-prevalence re-scoring. It shows LLM precision barely above always-flag (2.3–3.8% vs 2.21%).
   258	  - **C1:** the argmax-collapse artefact (p. 11) suggests some reports of fine-tuned models being near chance on PrimeVul may reflect decision thresholds rather than ranking ability. This is a candidate explanation, not established for other papers.
   259	  - **C4:** reasoning raises frontier balanced accuracy but lowers DeepSeek's specificity (more false positives) (p. 17, p. 20). This supports the "model-dependent" reading.
